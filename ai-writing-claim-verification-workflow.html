Verify AI-Assisted Marketing Claims Before Publishing

A practical claim ledger and source-checking workflow for AI-assisted marketing guides, with a fictional worked example and release checklist.

Scope: Educational guidance, not a promise of rankings, traffic, subscribers or income. No purchase or email subscription is required.

Why fluent writing is not evidence

A writing assistant can produce a persuasive paragraph without checking whether its numbers, quotations or product details are real. Treat generated prose as an unverified draft. A claim such as “this tool doubles newsletter conversions” requires a defined population, measurement period, method and source. If those cannot be established, delete or narrow the claim. An attractive chart or precise percentage does not make unsupported data reliable. This workflow is designed for small educational sites where a single inaccurate claim can mislead readers.

Separate claims by verification risk

Highlight every statement that a reader might reasonably rely on. Mark each as: (A) directly observable in your own current page or test, (B) supported by a primary published source, (C) interpretation or recommendation, or (D) unverified. Flag pricing, merchant policies, commissions, legal requirements, product compatibility, traffic statistics, search ranking claims and earnings statements for additional review. Opinions should be identified as judgments; hypothetical examples must be labeled fictional. Do not turn a merchant's promotional assertion into independent proof.

Use a source-and-scope ledger

For each material claim record: exact wording; source URL or reproducible test; source publisher; checked date; scope (country, product tier, period, sample); confidence; and action. Prefer current official documentation for product features and policies, actual contract terms for billing, and first-hand reproducible checks for page behavior. When an independent source contradicts a vendor, investigate the disagreement instead of choosing whichever number sounds better. A source that discusses paid search in one country cannot automatically justify a claim about organic traffic everywhere.

Fictional worked example

Draft claim: “All email platforms provide automatic double opt-in and guarantee higher deliverability.” Review: “all” and “guarantee” are unsupported; double opt-in availability and configuration vary, and deliverability depends on several factors. Revised educational statement: “Check whether your chosen provider supports a confirmation step, how it is configured, and whether it meets your consent requirements. A confirmation step can help document subscriber intent, but does not guarantee inbox placement.” This is an illustrative editing exercise, not a tested comparison of named providers.

Verify the destination, not just the anchor

If a guide links to a service, inspect the final landing page and its redirects. Check the current name, availability, pricing/currency where relevant, disclosure requirements and whether the page actually supports the surrounding sentence. For affiliate links, disclose the commercial relationship near the recommendation and use rel="sponsored nofollow noopener noreferrer" on the link. Do not add a referral merely because it exists in an inventory. For checkout links, verify product identity, deliverables, fulfillment, refund terms and buyer support before presenting a purchase button.

Publication and correction protocol

Before publishing, have a reviewer read the final rendered page, not only the source draft. Confirm that references open, quotations are accurately attributed, numbers can be recalculated, and a beginner can distinguish facts from examples. Save the claim ledger with a review date. If an error is discovered later, correct the affected text and note the material change; do not silently rewrite a misleading promise into a different promise. Review time-sensitive claims again when their source or merchant terms change.

Copyable claim-check worksheet

Record one row per claim: Claim | Type A/B/C/D | Evidence link or test | Scope | Checked date | Risk if wrong | Decision (keep, qualify, remove, recheck). Release only after all high-risk claims have a documented decision. For a five-minute spot check, sample the headline, first paragraph, every number, every quotation and every commercial recommendation. A passing spot check is not a substitute for full editorial review, but it identifies common failure points.
