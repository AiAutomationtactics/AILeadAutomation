Affiliate Disclosure and Marketing Claims Audit

A visitor should be able to tell when you may benefit from a recommendation. A truthful disclosure does not make an unsupported claim true, so both the link and its surrounding wording need review.

Written as a practical educational resource. It is not a guarantee of traffic, customers or income.

Disclose material relationships near the recommendation

If you may earn a commission from a link, say so in plain language before or near the relevant link. Do not rely solely on a footer. In markup, mark qualifying outbound promotional links with rel='sponsored nofollow'. The technical attribute supports transparency but does not replace an actual reader-facing disclosure.

Check every numerical promise

Statements like 'earn $1,000 tomorrow', '50,000 real buyers', and 'worth $5,000' need evidence and context. If an offer has variable results, describe what it teaches or delivers rather than asserting typical income or outcomes. Explain when a number is only an illustrative calculation.

Separate reviews from marketing pages

Do not write first-hand reviews if you did not personally test a product. Identify independent comparisons, vendor-supplied details, and your own observations separately. Avoid invented testimonials, star ratings, false deadlines, fake scarcity, or unauthorized use of recognizable brands and logos.

Keep the user in control

Links should open their stated destination; pages should not hijack the back button, launch unexpected sales pages, auto-enroll visitors, or conceal recurring charges. Confirm prices and terms at the destination because affiliate programs change. If you cannot verify a claim, revise or remove it before distributing the link.

Try this exercise

Audit one existing promotion. Highlight every claim about money, number of users, price, speed, traffic quality, or guaranteed results. For each highlight, keep a source or rewrite it as a neutral description of the actual resource.
