Plan a Useful YouTube Tutorial Without Filming People

A helpful educational video does not require a person on camera. It does require a clear demonstration, readable visuals, accurate narration and a useful next step.

What you will learn: A practical storyboard and production checklist for screen recordings, slides, captions, narration and honest descriptions.

Pick one action a viewer can complete

A video called “Every way to get website traffic” is difficult to teach in five minutes. “How to label a campaign link and check that it opens correctly” is manageable. The narrower promise lets you demonstrate a result instead of filling time with generic slides.

Write the desired outcome before opening editing software. For example: “After this video, a beginner can create a tagged link, test it in a private browser window, and record its campaign name.” If the viewer would still need to guess at a crucial step, the script is incomplete.

A six-minute storyboard

  • 0:00–0:25 — Show the finished result. Display a sample tracking link and the simple campaign log it belongs to. State that tracking is imperfect and does not guarantee sales.
  • 0:25–1:10 — Explain the prerequisite. Define source, medium and campaign using one example rather than a list of marketing jargon.
  • 1:10–3:20 — Demonstrate the process. Build the link on screen. Zoom in on the fields being changed. Pause long enough for viewers to read.
  • 3:20–4:30 — Test and troubleshoot. Open the link, check the destination and explain what to do if parameters disappear.
  • 4:30–5:30 — Summarize the checklist. Repeat the three actions in the order the viewer should perform them.
  • 5:30–6:00 — Offer a relevant next step. Point to a free worksheet or a related guide, not an unrelated purchase page.

These timings are an example, not a required platform formula. A shorter video is better if it completes the task.

Use visuals you have rights to publish

A clean slide deck, your own screen recording, simple icons, and annotated diagrams can work without people appearing in the video. Avoid showing private emails, customer records, passwords, browser bookmarks, or API tokens. Use a demonstration account or blur sensitive material before exporting.

Do not copy another creator's footage, logo, paid course slides, or copyrighted music without permission. Silence or a clear narration is preferable to music that competes with instructions. If you use a synthetic voice, review pronunciation and timing; a natural-sounding voice does not excuse factual errors.

Make the video understandable without sound

Create accurate captions, not merely auto-generated text left unchecked. Put key actions in on-screen labels such as “Step 2: Check destination URL.” Use readable contrast, avoid tiny screenshots, and leave enough time for viewers to follow the cursor. Describe important visual changes in the narration so a listener does not have to infer them.

For an educational thumbnail, show the actual problem and a legible outcome: “UTM LINK CHECK” alongside a simplified URL field. Do not add fake analytics graphs, fabricated revenue figures, or a misleading countdown.

Write a description that matches the lesson

The first two sentences should explain exactly what is taught. Then list the main steps and a relevant resource. If the description contains an affiliate link, disclose that you may earn a commission near that link; where a disclosure is required in the video itself, include it there too. The FTC's endorsement guidance explains why a disclosure buried away from a recommendation may be inadequate.

A description should not repeat dozens of unrelated keywords or affiliate URLs. A viewer who clicks for a tracking tutorial should reach a tracking tutorial.

Quality-control pass before publishing

Watch the export on a phone at normal size. Confirm that the text can be read, the cursor is visible, captions match the spoken words, and no private information appears. Follow the tutorial as if you were a beginner. Check that every link in the description opens the stated destination.

Record the video title, production date, source materials, and what was actually tested. If a tool interface changes, update the description or replace the outdated segment. Do not claim a tutorial is current solely because you changed its upload date.

A one-video exercise

Choose one of your existing guides, such as the UTM link builder. Create a six-scene storyboard with one sentence of narration and one visual per scene. Remove any scene that does not help the viewer complete the task. This yields a more useful video than producing many interchangeable promotional clips.
