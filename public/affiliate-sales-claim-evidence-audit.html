Verify Affiliate Sales-Page Claims Before Repeating Them

A step-by-step claim audit for product features, price, refund terms, testimonials and earnings statements before affiliate publication.

Home / Verify Affiliate Sales-Page Claims Before Repeating Them

Educational guide. Examples are fictional; no earnings, indexing, conversion or traffic outcome is promised. No products are endorsed here.

Treat every material claim as a testable question

Sales copy often mixes factual descriptions, estimates and opinions. Turn each statement into a specific question: Is the price current? Does a refund apply to this purchase? Is the product delivered immediately? Is the advertised feature included in the listed plan? What conditions apply? If the claim could materially influence a purchase, it deserves evidence before you repeat it.

Separate four evidence types

Primary product documentation can establish what a vendor currently says; terms and checkout pages can establish stated contractual conditions; a reproducible hands-on test can establish what you personally observed; independent reviews can add context but may be biased or outdated. None of these proves future results. Record source URL, capture date, product version and the exact scope of the evidence. Never turn a vendor's assertion into your own verified experience.

Audit price and currency at the point of decision

Check the visible checkout price, currency, tax treatment, shipping or digital delivery fees, subscription renewals, trial conversion, upsells and cancellation terms. A headline price may apply only to an initial term. If a checkout requires login or is not accessible, mark the price unverified. Do not publish a 'today only' price unless the deadline and terms are independently confirmed. For international readers, currency conversion and taxes can change the total.

Read refund conditions rather than summarizing from memory

Locate the actual refund policy for the exact product and seller. Record deadlines, eligibility, exclusions, support steps and whether the policy covers recurring payments or add-ons. Distinguish a vendor's stated policy from your personal experience receiving a refund. If no clear policy is available, say so instead of inventing 'money-back guaranteed'. Do not conflate a payment processor's dispute process with a merchant refund promise.

Test product and delivery statements cautiously

If you have lawful access to the product, document which features you used, the environment and the date. A screenshot of a dashboard is not proof that a promised automation performs well. For software, note dependencies, required integrations, account limits and failure cases. For educational downloads, verify file format, completeness, accessibility and support contact. Without access, write a vendor-description summary with explicit uncertainty instead of a hands-on review.

Evaluate testimonials and earnings claims

Ask whether testimonials are authentic, representative, current and properly authorized for reuse. Do not copy private customer information. An earnings screenshot without net costs, time period, sample size and attribution is not a reliable expected result. Avoid guaranteed-income statements or numerical projections unless robust substantiation exists, and do not suggest typical results from an exceptional anecdote. Readers benefit more from transparent cost and risk explanations.

Fictional worked audit

Imagine a fictional landing page saying 'Unlimited visitors, $19 one time, instant results, full refund.' The evidence ledger might show the checkout lists $19 in USD but also an optional recurring upgrade, the vendor defines 'unlimited' as subject to fair-use limits, and the refund policy excludes some delivered services. A careful article would describe the actual verified terms and omit 'instant results'. This is a hypothetical illustration, not a review of a real vendor.

Build a pre-publication table

For each claim record: exact proposed wording; why it matters; primary source; date checked; verified, qualified or unsupported; final published wording; and next review trigger. Example: 'One-time purchase' → checkout and terms → only verified for base plan → 'Base plan is listed as a one-time payment; optional upgrades may have different terms.' If evidence is absent, do not insert a price or checkout button.

Disclose the commercial relationship clearly

Place an affiliate disclosure close to any endorsement, not hidden behind a generic footer link. A practical disclosure is: 'I may earn a commission if you purchase through this link, at no additional cost to you.' Use rel='sponsored nofollow noopener noreferrer' on qualifying links. A disclosure does not cure an unsupported claim. Keep the educational worksheet accessible without mandatory email submission.

Maintain corrections and expiration checks

Set review triggers for broken destinations, checkout changes, new product versions, policy changes and reader-reported discrepancies. If a material claim becomes unsupported, remove or qualify it promptly and record the correction date. Track the difference between an affiliate click, a completed checkout and an approved commission; none can be inferred from the other. The safest publication decision may be to defer the review until key facts can be verified.
