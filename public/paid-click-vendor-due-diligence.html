Before Buying Visitor Clicks: A Vendor Due-Diligence Worksheet

A practical checklist for assessing paid-click vendors, traffic definitions, refund terms, tracking quality and audience fit before risking a small budget.

Home / Before Buying Visitor Clicks: A Vendor Due-Diligence Worksheet

Educational guidance only. All numerical examples are fictional. No traffic, leads, earnings, rankings or investment results are guaranteed.

Begin with the visitor's reason for arriving

A vendor can deliver a counted click without delivering a person interested in your lesson. Ask what a click means in the vendor's reporting: a link activation, a redirect request, a loaded destination, or a unique human visitor? These are not equivalent. Some visits may be accidental, automated, repeated, or poorly matched to the advertised topic. If the vendor cannot describe the source and counting method, treat that as a reason not to buy. A legitimate test starts with a useful page and a clearly defined audience, not a large advertised number.

Request the source and placement details

Ask where advertisements appear, whether placements are opt-in, whether visitors are incentivized to click, whether geographic and topic targeting are available, and whether the traffic includes pop-unders, auto-refreshes or traffic exchanges. Request a representative example placement before purchase. Ask whether the service allows educational affiliate content, whether your landing page complies with its rules, and whether its claims can be substantiated. A seller's description is evidence of what they promise, not proof of how the traffic will perform.

Separate reporting units

Write down five distinct measurements: vendor-reported clicks; server requests; analytics sessions; provider-confirmed voluntary subscribers; and actual approved net commissions. A redirect can be counted by the vendor while the destination fails to load. Browser privacy settings, consent, time zones and filtering can cause legitimate differences between tools. A mismatch is a question to investigate, not automatic proof of fraud. Keep the raw counts, report dates and definitions side by side. Never equate a form button click with an accepted or confirmed email subscription.

Inspect commercial terms before payment

Check the exact currency, tax treatment, total charge, minimum purchase, recurring billing, renewal and cancellation method, delivery window, unused-credit expiry, and refund policy. Save a dated copy of the offer terms and receipt. Look for clear contact information and a support path. Do not rely on a vague claim of guaranteed visitors, rankings, sales or earnings. If the price or refund conditions are unclear, postpone the purchase. A zero-cost organic test can be a better way to validate whether the page actually helps readers.

Use a fictional comparison

Suppose Vendor A offers 300 reported clicks for CAD $15, while Vendor B offers 100 reported clicks for CAD $12. Vendor A appears cheaper per reported click: 15 ÷ 300 = CAD $0.05, versus 12 ÷ 100 = CAD $0.12. That arithmetic does not establish that A is better. If A cannot explain where visits originate, its lower price offers little decision value. If B supplies placement details and clearer cancellation terms, B may be easier to audit, but neither is proven to produce qualified leads. The figures are invented, not vendor quotations.

Build a purchase/no-purchase gate

Record the exact landing-page URL, target reader, one useful action, maximum affordable loss, planned start and end date, and stop condition. Confirm that the page works on a phone and keyboard, loads without misleading redirects, and explains email consent. Test analytics with your own visits and label those tests. If the vendor cannot provide source details, if terms are missing, or if you cannot afford to lose the entire amount, choose no purchase. Never borrow or use essential living funds to finance an uncertain traffic test.

Audit results without changing the question

After a test, reconcile vendor counts with landing-page observations and provider-confirmed outcomes. Note that one visitor may start multiple sessions and some subscribers may arrive from other channels. Check whether campaign tags survived redirects, whether the destination changed, and whether the audience was relevant. Record both what was observed and what remains unknown. Do not extrapolate a commission from a handful of visits. A failed test may identify a broken link, confusing headline or poor audience match; it does not justify buying more clicks to recover sunk costs.
