Build a 90-Day SEO Maintenance Plan Without Publishing More Pages

A good maintenance cycle makes existing lessons more accurate, accessible and useful. It does not require five new URLs every day. This plan works for small educational sites, including those with GitHub source and separate production hosting.

Start with an inventory, not a publishing quota

Export a list of your site's actual canonical pages from the CMS or repository, then compare it with pages that return HTTP 200 on the production host. Record URL, reader task, owner, last substantive update, indexability, canonical target, internal links, and any important outbound references. A GitHub file or an entry in a sitemap is not evidence that a page is deployed. For each URL, verify the live response and the page's rendered robots directive. If production cannot be reached, label the audit blocked rather than silently marking pages healthy.

Use four decisions: keep, improve, consolidate, retire

Keep a page when it answers a distinct reader question accurately and remains accessible. Improve it when examples, steps, links or terminology are stale but the reader task remains useful. Consolidate when two pages answer substantially the same question and one clear resource can satisfy both; plan relevant redirects only after verifying the replacement and hosting controls. Retire only when the content is obsolete, misleading, legally problematic or genuinely without a useful replacement. Low traffic alone is not proof that a page has no value. Do not bulk-delete articles simply because a search dashboard reports zero clicks.

Days 1–15: establish a reliable baseline

Choose a manageable sample: the homepage, primary learning paths, all pages with known errors, and the ten pages most important to reader tasks. Check mobile navigation by keyboard, heading order, link text, form labels, contrast, image alternatives, broken downloads, and clear attribution for statistics. Record actual status codes for live pages, including 404s, redirects and temporary server errors. Compare the page title and description with what the article actually teaches. Note the date and exact URL of each observation; do not claim a Google indexing change based only on your own fetch.

Days 16–30: correct trust and measurement defects

Prioritize broken instructions, inaccessible controls, misleading commercial claims, duplicated signup events, incorrect analytics definitions and unclear email consent before cosmetic SEO changes. If a guide discusses an offer, independently recheck product features, billing, refund terms and merchant permissions before repeating them. Remove unsupported earnings claims. Put an understandable affiliate disclosure close to a relevant commercial recommendation, and mark promotional external links rel="sponsored nofollow noopener noreferrer". Keep checkout links off unrelated education pages. An email form should explain the sender, purpose, optional nature of subscribing, and unsubscribe process; never disguise a marketing signup as required access to a public tutorial.

Days 31–45: improve the paths readers actually take

Choose three reader journeys such as beginner research → measurement basics → a troubleshooting worksheet. Confirm that each link leads to a page serving the next task, not merely a page sharing the same keyword. Replace vague anchors such as “click here” with the destination's purpose. Fix orphaned educational pages when they belong in a genuine navigation path. Avoid a network-wide footer of promotional links; the quantity of links is not evidence of helpfulness. Test navigation on narrow screens and with a keyboard.

Days 46–60: review evidence before changing search metadata

For each candidate page, record the search question it answers, its opening explanation, evidence and examples. Update a title or description only when it more accurately represents the content; do not promise a ranking lift. Verify canonical URLs against the intended deployed version. A staging article with no verified live production URL should remain noindex and absent from a public sitemap. The sitemap should list only canonical, live, indexable pages; lastmod should change for meaningful content revisions, not for routine builds. Keep a deliberately private or review-only link archive noindex.

Days 61–75: consolidate with a rollback plan

Before merging overlapping articles, write down what distinct questions each serves. If a useful difference remains, keep both and clarify their scopes. Otherwise choose a strong destination, incorporate genuinely useful unique material, preserve important citations, and verify that any planned redirect points to the correct live page. Test old URLs after deployment. Maintain a rollback copy and record any loss of functionality. Do not add a redirect to an unrelated affiliate landing page: a reader seeking a tutorial should still receive a tutorial.

Days 76–90: validate outcomes and choose the next cycle

Compare the same measurement definitions and comparable windows. Look at confirmed form submissions rather than treating ad-network clicks as subscribers. Inspect errors, reader questions, completed worksheet actions and whether fixes remained stable. Record traffic source and device changes that could explain observed differences. A rise from 2 to 3 leads is one additional lead, not evidence of a predictable 50% business improvement. Finish with a short decision log: maintain, repair, consolidate, investigate or leave unchanged. Schedule another review based on risk and available time, not an arbitrary page quota.

A realistic 30-minute weekly review

Minutes 0–5: check known broken links, form delivery and unresolved critical defects. Minutes 5–12: review one reader task and its first-screen explanation. Minutes 12–20: test two internal journeys and one outbound factual citation. Minutes 20–25: inspect a small set of consistently defined analytics observations, noting missing data. Minutes 25–30: record one prioritized fix, its owner and the verification method. If a critical accessibility, consent or factual defect appears, spend the whole session fixing it instead. The routine is a triage tool, not a substitute for a complete periodic audit.

Worked example: choose an update instead of another article

Imagine a fictional site has a 2025 “What counts as a lead?” article and a 2026 “Count newsletter signups” article. The first defines ad clicks as leads, while the second distinguishes clicks, form starts and provider-confirmed subscribers. The useful action is to correct the first article, link to the second for implementation detail, and remove the misleading definition. A third article repeating the same distinction adds little. Document the exact changed paragraphs, verify both URLs after deployment and update lastmod only for the genuinely revised article.

Copyable maintenance register and stop conditions

For each page, capture: URL ____; reader task ____; live HTTP result/date ____; canonical ____; robots directive ____; accessibility issue ____; factual source to recheck ____; commercial disclosure needed? ____; broken link ____; proposed decision ____; owner ____; actual content-change date ____; post-deploy verification ____; Search Console evidence if authorized ____. Stop publication when the domain serves a different site, DNS fails, the canonical cannot be confirmed, consent is broken, or a material product claim is unverified. A commit alone never clears these gates.
