An Editorial Quality Scorecard for AI-Assisted SEO Content

Before adding a new SEO article, test whether it gives readers something they could not get from a generic list. This scorecard makes that decision concrete.

Educational material drafted with AI assistance and reviewed for relevance. Examples are illustrative unless expressly identified as observed. No results are guaranteed.

A 10-point reader-value review

Give one point for each yes answer: (1) the page targets a real question; (2) its first screen answers that question; (3) it includes a specific worked example; (4) it offers a usable checklist or worksheet; (5) it describes a meaningful limitation; (6) its facts and dates are checked; (7) its headings describe the content accurately; (8) related internal links are genuinely useful; (9) endorsements and referrals are disclosed; and (10) it has been checked on a phone.

These ten points are an internal publishing aid—not a Google algorithm or official ranking score. A low score is a signal to improve the draft, not to add more keywords.

Apply it to a real beginner task

Suppose an article promises “Best affiliate traffic sources.” Without a defined audience, it cannot give a meaningful answer. Narrow it to “How a new creator with no advertising budget can choose one audience channel.” Demonstrate how a YouTube explanation might support search intent while a forum answer requires more participation and stricter community rules.

Add a one-week plan: identify ten recurring questions, make one original tutorial, publish one compliant reply, record genuinely engaged visits, and evaluate whether the audience asked for follow-up content. The plan becomes usable even if the reader never clicks a referral.

Check evidence and freshness

Separate evergreen concepts from facts that change. A definition of conversion rate may not need a fresh source; an ad price, commission percentage, platform policy or claim about a service does. Where possible, link to the original official page. If you lack evidence, write “not independently verified” rather than treating a merchant's sales copy as proof.

For comparison pages, disclose the criteria you use. If you haven't bought or tested a product, don't use phrases such as “our hands-on testing proves.”

Quality gate for affiliate recommendations

Affiliate links should come after the reader's decision framework, not replace it. Ask: does this link provide a relevant next step, have we described the relationship, and have we made clear what we do not know? An external link should be descriptive and marked rel="sponsored" if it represents an affiliate relationship.

The affiliate disclosure guide contains examples. The saved links archive is intentionally noindexed because a giant list alone is not an editorial recommendation.

Publish, improve, or merge

Decision rule: if a draft scores eight or more and its primary question is not already addressed, it may be ready after a human accuracy check. If it scores five to seven, revise the example and missing safeguards. If it scores below five or repeats an existing page, merge its unique information into that page. The threshold is our editorial choice only.

After publication, revisit the guide when you discover reader confusion, a broken link, or changed guidance. Do not update dates without updating the substance.
