Write a YouTube Description That Matches the Tutorial

A description should help someone decide whether a video solves their problem, then find the demonstrated steps and supporting material. It is not a second sales page.

Home / Write a YouTube Description That Matches the Tutorial
In this guide: A worked description, a claim-by-claim accuracy audit, and a simple procedure for checking every linked resource.

Start with a promise you can prove on screen

Before writing the description, watch the exported video and list what a viewer can actually accomplish. A tutorial that demonstrates adding campaign parameters to a URL can promise a sample tracking link and a manual destination check. It cannot promise that the viewer will get more sales, that every analytics platform will attribute a visit correctly, or that the link will automatically be indexed.

Write a one-sentence outcome in plain language: “In this tutorial, you will label a campaign URL, test its destination, and record the settings in a campaign log.” Compare each verb against the recording. If the video only explains the idea but never shows a test, change “test” to “learn how to test” or record the missing step.

Use a four-part description, in reader order

  1. Outcome: The first two sentences name the task and the audience. Avoid a vague hook that hides what the video teaches.
  2. Sequence: A short list of the steps actually shown. Add timestamps only after confirming them against the final export.
  3. Resources: Link to the exact worksheet, documentation, or guide mentioned. Explain what is available at the destination and whether it requires an email address.
  4. Context: State meaningful limits, any sponsorship or affiliate relationship, and the date of a time-sensitive demonstration.

Use ordinary words rather than repeating a search phrase in every line. The description should remain useful when read aloud by a screen reader. A video title and thumbnail must also match the content; a precise description cannot repair a misleading promise elsewhere.

Worked example: a tracking-link lesson

Suppose the video really shows how to create a link with utm_source, utm_medium and utm_campaign, then checks that the destination loads. A draft description could read:

Example video description — fictional lesson, not an uploaded video

“Learn to label a campaign URL for a small educational website. I show how to choose consistent UTM values, open the finished link, and record the test in a simple log.

00:00 — What a tagged URL does
00:34 — Choose source, medium and campaign
01:48 — Add the parameters
03:10 — Test the destination
04:05 — Record the result and limitations

Companion guide: Build a campaign tracking link. It explains the naming fields and includes a local example.

Analytics reports may differ because tools define events differently and some visits are not measured. This lesson does not promise traffic or revenue.”

These timestamps are illustrative placeholders; do not publish them unchanged unless the finished video matches. Notice that the example provides one relevant resource instead of ten unrelated referral links.

Audit the links and claims before upload

Open every URL in a fresh browser session. Confirm the destination title, HTTPS connection, visible content, and whether the page asks for payment or personal information. If a resource requires signup, say so before the click. A link that worked during scripting may later redirect to an unrelated offer; recheck when updating the video.

For paid recommendations, explain the relationship close to the link in understandable words such as “I may earn a commission if you buy through this link.” Do not hide that disclosure behind a generic “resources” label. Check applicable platform and advertising rules for disclosure in the video as well as its description. This example deliberately contains no affiliate links.

Platform behavior to check, not assume

According to YouTube's current link-sharing help, clickable external URLs in long-form video descriptions require the relevant advanced features, while URLs in Shorts descriptions and comments are not clickable. Do not tell a Shorts viewer to “tap the link below” unless that specific link placement is actually interactive. Verify your own channel's current options before publishing.

Ten-minute publishing audit

  • Read the description while the final video plays. Every claimed demonstration must be visible.
  • Verify that the first two sentences answer “What will I learn?” without an exaggerated result.
  • Check each timestamp and remove any chapter marker that points to the wrong scene.
  • Open the linked resource on a phone; identify any signup, fee or missing file.
  • Proofread captions, product names and URL text; remove private data from screenshots.
  • Confirm the disclosure is beside any promotional recommendation and not buried after a wall of hashtags.
  • Keep a dated note of the title, description and link destinations so future edits are traceable.

For a final exercise, copy one of your existing descriptions into two columns: statement made and evidence visible in the video. Delete or qualify any statement that has no supporting scene. This produces a more trustworthy description than simply adding more keywords.
