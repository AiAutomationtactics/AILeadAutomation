Plan a Consent-Based Three-Email Welcome Sequence

Email is useful only when recipients understand why they receive messages and can leave easily. A welcome sequence should begin with the promised help, not a flood of unrelated affiliate offers.

Written as a practical educational resource. It is not a guarantee of traffic, customers or income.

Email one: deliver what you promised

Subject example: 'Your traffic planning worksheet'. Thank the subscriber, provide a working link to the resource, and explain what to expect next. If a signup was requested to access a checklist, do not substitute a sales page. Include a sender identity and a clearly visible unsubscribe mechanism supported by your mailing provider.

Email two: teach a small improvement

Subject example: 'One way to check traffic quality'. Explain one measurement that a beginner can reproduce, such as comparing tracked visits with completed opt-ins. State any limits. Invite readers to reply with a question if reply handling is actually configured. Avoid fabricating success stories or pushing a different offer in every paragraph.

Email three: offer a relevant next step

Subject example: 'Choose your next traffic experiment'. Share a practical experiment and, only if genuinely helpful, one related third-party resource. Tell readers when the destination is an affiliate or sponsored link. Avoid claims of guaranteed commissions. Make the educational material complete even if someone does not click the promotion.

Maintain trust after the sequence

Use clear signup consent, keep evidence of how permission was obtained, and honor unsubscribes. Review applicable marketing email requirements for your audience and mailing service. Track high-level delivery and engagement without using sensitive data in URL tags. Remove broken links and obsolete instructions when programs change.

Try this exercise

Write the three subjects and a single main teaching point for each. Read them as if you received them unexpectedly: can you tell who sent them, why, and how to stop them?
